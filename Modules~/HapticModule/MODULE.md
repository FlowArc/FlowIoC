# Haptic

## Purpose
Plays the nine haptic presets on iOS and Android through the platforms' own APIs, and keeps the
player's on/off choice.

## Concepts
haptic, haptics, vibration, vibrate, rumble, tactile feedback, impact, preset, IHapticService,
HapticPreset

## Decisions
- **Own backend, no vendor.** HitNPoP's module used three calls of Nice Vibrations - `Init`,
  `hapticsEnabled`, `PlayPreset` - and read at the Lofelt source those are UIKit's feedback
  generators on iOS and `VibrationEffect.createWaveform` on Android. The asset is archived, carries
  15 MB of native binaries, and a second copy collides with the one Feel installs. So `FlowHaptics.mm`
  makes the same UIKit calls, `AndroidHapticPlayer` makes the same Vibrator calls through JNI, and
  `HapticPresetEnvelopes` carries Lofelt's nine tables (MIT) with its 25 ms interpolation
  reproduced in `HapticWaveformBuilder` - in `float` arithmetic, narrowed at every step, because the
  core's `f32` rounding decides the last amplitude bit and the tests pin the core's output.
- **Medium runs 120 ms, the one cell that is not Lofelt's 80.** Felt on a OnePlus CPH2747
  (Android 16): its firmware performs every waveform step of about 100 ms or under as its own
  45 ms click and scales only the strength (`OplusPrebakedSegment` in `dumpsys vibrator_manager`),
  so Lofelt's Light, Selection, Medium and Rigid were one click at three strengths, while the
  160 ms Heavy and Soft played as real waveforms. Played back to back from the shell, a Medium of
  120 ms stood apart from Light and from Heavy; 110 ms also crossed that threshold but leaves no
  margin for another firmware's. Light and Selection stay one click apart in strength there, by
  design: pushing Selection over the threshold would make a tick a buzz. The Android 10
  predefined effects were measured on the same phone and gained nothing - TICK felt as Light,
  CLICK weaker than Medium, HEAVY_CLICK a double hit - and it reports no Android 12 primitives,
  so neither road is taken.
- **The interface is the surface.** `IHapticService.Play(preset)` is called from the game's own
  Command at the point where it decided something happened, which is where the choice of preset
  belongs. No `Scripts/Signals`: nothing for a Connector to hang a preset off.
- **The on/off choice is the module's**, in PlayerPrefs under `flowioc.haptic.enabled`, default on.
  Installed, it works; a settings screen reads `IsEnabled()` and dispatches to a Command that calls
  `SetEnabled`.
- **The `VIBRATE` permission is added by the module** after Gradle generation
  (`HapticGradleProjectPostProcessor`, an `IPostGenerateGradleAndroidProject` editing the
  unityLibrary manifest), once, whatever the game or another plugin already declared. Unity 6's
  typed `AndroidProjectFilesModifier` was tried first and cannot edit that manifest - it only adds
  files of its own. Nothing to add to a manifest by hand.
- **The Editor never vibrates.** `SilentHapticPlayer` logs `Play - <preset>` on the module's channel
  so a flow reads in the Flow Console; a device build is the only place a preset is felt, and the
  test scene cannot go there because a test module is Editor-only.

## Known gaps
- The iOS side has not been built: `FlowHaptics.mm` was written against Lofelt's `SystemHaptics.m`
  on a Windows machine. The first Xcode build is its test.
- Presets only. No `.haptic` clips, no amplitude or frequency by hand, no gamepad rumble.
- Android plays the envelopes; iOS plays the system haptic of the same name and cannot be tuned.
